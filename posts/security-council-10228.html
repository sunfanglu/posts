Security Council, 10228th meeting: artificial intelligence and international security

26 September 2026 · Fanglu Sun

This note follows the published reading of the United Nations Security Council’s 10228th meeting on artificial intelligence and international security. Nineteen speakers took part: four briefers and all fifteen Council members.

Almost everyone acknowledges that AI has benefits. What remains unresolved is who may set the pace of frontier models, who bears the risks, and whether the Security Council should be the body that deals with this.

Three fault lines

  • Pace of the frontier. The scientific panel and some labs want safety evidence to stay ahead of capability. The United States rejects a pause and global speed limits. Several Global South countries agree that the risks cross borders, but reject a rule under which a few companies and states decide who must slow down.
  • Choice of forum. One side wants loss of control, biological misuse, and lethal autonomous weapons kept within the Council’s field of view. The other side insists on specialized bodies, domestic law, and intergovernmental consensus, so that the Council does not become a technology regulator.
  • Who is left out. If the language of catastrophic risk is written only by frontier states, it will be read as a new technology threshold. Access, production, open source, and mineral value chains therefore become security questions, not only development questions.
